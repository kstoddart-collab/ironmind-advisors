The gap nobody measures

The transition doesn't fail on skill. It fails on operating system.

For twenty-two years an institution told you what to do, when to do it, and how well you did it. Every measurement you ever received was academic. Then it stopped — no syllabus, no schedule, no grade — and the silence is loud.

What breaks in the first three years out is almost never intelligence or credentials. It is the absence of an operating system: the ability to find the work without being assigned it, run your own money, build a network once school stops handing you one, and hold a standard nobody is enforcing.

Those things are trainable. They are also, until now, unmeasured — which is why so many capable twenty-four-year-olds know something is wrong and have no vocabulary for it. The Transition Readiness Score gives you the vocabulary, the number, and the sequence.

Can you stand up · Do you know where you're going · Will you still be standing in two years.

The measurement

Eight domains, one hundred points each

Every domain is scored independently, so you see exactly where strength concentrates and where the next ninety days go.

01

Workplace Operating System

How fluently you function inside a professional environment — its unwritten rules, its tempo, and the difference between what you were assigned and what the job actually requires.

02

Financial Self-Command

Running your own money — income, obligations, buffer, and long-term position — without a parent, a loan, or a credit line quietly making the decisions for you.

03

Identity Beyond the Transcript

How settled your sense of self is once school, GPA, and student status stop telling you who you are. The load-bearing wall of the entire profile.

04

Purpose & Direction

Whether you are steering or drifting — and whether the thing you are chasing would still be worth chasing if nobody found out you got it.

05

Professional Voice & Presence

Being understood, taken seriously, and able to advocate for yourself in rooms full of people who are older, more senior, and don't already know you.

06

Resilience & Regulation

Recovery speed under rejection, criticism, and ambiguity — and whether the habits that keep you functional hold up when you're under load, not just when you're not.

07

Relational Capital

The network you build on purpose once the built-in one from school is gone. The most underrated domain on the assessment, and the one that pays back fastest.

08

Ownership & Standards

The internal code that governs you when no one is assigning, watching, or grading. The multiplier — it decides whether the other seven compound or plateau.

The ladder

Five levels of authorship

Your composite score places you on a five-rung ladder. Every rung is honest work — the arc runs from learning a trade someone else defined to writing the terms yourself.

200–499The Apprentice

Learning the trade of adulthood. Honest work, just begun.

500–699The Builder

Structure is going up. Some systems hold; some are still scaffolding.

700–849The Operator

You run your own life day to day. Refinement is the work.

850–949The Architect

You design the life rather than react to it.

950–1000The Author

Standards, direction, and voice are yours. You set terms.
